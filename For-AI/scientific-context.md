# Scientific context for the current game

Last updated: 2026-09-29

A blicket is an object with a hidden causal property. The player sees each object's appearance and the detector's response, then infers which objects have that property and how they combine. The current scenario has three distinct three-object sets in a fixed disjunctive, disjunctive, conjunctive order. It is a playable adaptation, not a validated research instrument.

Keep visible appearance separate from hidden truth in the scenario. Show the same checking motion for every complete one- or two-object subset; only after the check does gold blink mark activation or red blink mark non-activation. These colors are digital feedback choices for this PC game. Record each tested subset and each object judgment. The platform lowering is a digital checking cue, not a claim about every physical Blicket apparatus.

Lucas et al. (2014, *Cognition*, DOI: 10.1016/j.cognition.2013.12.010) compared disjunctive and conjunctive training between conditions, then tested transfer to a new ambiguous set. Its training evidence has the same six outcomes used here: OR gives A+, B−, C+, AB+, AC+, BC+; AND gives A−, B−, C−, AB−, AC+, BC−. The fixed OR→OR→AND sequence within one session is the requested digital adaptation. It omits the publication's separate ambiguous transfer test. Do not describe it as a replication or validated instrument.

All task objects share matte gray color, following gray ceramic/clay precedents in Lucas et al. (2014) and Wente et al. (2017 online; 2019 issue). This removes color differences, not shape, size, order, or spatial preferences. Counterbalance fixed shape-to-cause mappings and phase order before experimental use. Detector feedback colors are a separate digital design choice.

See the [German terminology and shapes brief](../docs/research/german-blicket-terminology-and-shapes.md) for evidence and proposed wording. Retain `Blicket` provisionally; no standardized German detector-task replacement or validated German instrument was found. Spanish `Flipo` and Dutch `Fonkel` are not established German terms. English and German on-screen text are implemented, but not participant validated. Earlier voice clips are dormant because their singular prompts conflict with the pair trials.
