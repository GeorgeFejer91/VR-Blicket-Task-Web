# Scientific context for the current game

Last updated: 2026-09-27

A blicket is an object with a hidden causal property that makes a detector activate. The player sees each object's appearance and the detector's response, then infers which object has that property. The current three-object scenario is a deterministic, single-object demonstration, derived from the repository's existing V1 scenario. It is a playable model of the task, not a validated research instrument.

Keep visible appearance separate from hidden truth in the scenario. Show the same checking motion for every object; only after the check does gold blink mark activation or red blink mark non-activation. These colors are digital feedback choices for this PC game. Record both the player's point choice and separate judgments. The platform lowering is a digital checking cue, not a claim about every physical Blicket apparatus.

Only reviewed public research summaries belong here. Do not expand to probabilistic, conjunctive, disjunctive, or counterbalanced designs until a specific protocol and validation need is chosen.

All task objects now share matte gray color, following gray ceramic/clay precedents in Lucas et al. (2014) and Wente et al. (2017 online; 2019 issue). This removes color differences, not shape, size, order, or spatial preferences. Counterbalance the fixed shape-to-cause mapping before experimental use. Detector feedback colors are a separate digital design choice.

See the [German terminology and shapes brief](../docs/research/german-blicket-terminology-and-shapes.md) for evidence and proposed wording. Retain `Blicket` provisionally; no standardized German detector-task replacement or validated German instrument was found. Spanish `Flipo` and Dutch `Fonkel` are not established German terms. The game remains English until localization is specifically implemented and reviewed.
